01 — OVERVIEW

The Problem

Users open Shazam, identify a song, and leave within 10 seconds. The app has become a pure utility—users get what they need and bounce. There's no reason to stay, explore, or return.

We set out to answer: How might we transform Shazam from a one-time lookup tool into an experience that keeps users engaged?

02 — THE CHALLENGE

Why users leave immediately after identifying songs

Through user interviews and affinity mapping, we uncovered the core issues preventing engagement.

No reason to stay

Users get what they need instantly. There's no value in exploring further because Shazam doesn't offer anything beyond identification.

Disconnected experience

After identifying a song, users must leave Shazam to actually listen on Spotify or Apple Music. The experience feels fragmented.

Missed emotional connection

Music is deeply personal and emotional, but Shazam treats it like data. Users want to feel something, not just see a result.

03 — SO WHAT?

Shazam needs to become more than a lookup tool

If Shazam doesn't evolve beyond song identification, users will continue to treat it as a forgettable utility. The opportunity is to make discovery feel magical—giving users a reason to return, explore, and engage with music in deeper ways.

04 — THE SOLUTION

Redesigning for discovery, connection, and retention

We introduced features that encourage users to stay, explore, and feel emotionally connected to their music discoveries.

06 — RESEARCH & PROCESS

How we got here

We conducted user interviews, created affinity maps, developed personas, and iterated through wireframes to validate our approach.

1

User Interviews

Conducted interviews with 4 frequent Shazam users to understand their behaviors, frustrations, and needs. Key finding: everyone exhibited the "identify and bounce" pattern.

2

Affinity Mapping

Organized interview insights into themes to identify patterns. This revealed three core problem areas: lack of retention features, fragmented experience, and missing emotional connection.

3

Persona Development

Created user personas representing our target audience—music enthusiasts who use Shazam regularly but never engage beyond identification.

4

Journey Mapping

Mapped the current user journey to identify pain points and opportunities. The biggest drop-off happened immediately after song identification.

5

Wireframing & Iteration

Created low-fidelity wireframes to test different retention strategies. Iterated based on feedback to arrive at our final feature set.

6

High-Fidelity Prototyping

Built interactive prototypes in Figma to demonstrate the redesigned experience and gather final validation.

07 — DESIGN PRINCIPLES

Our guiding principles

01

Make discovery feel magical

Every interaction should feel delightful and surprising. Music discovery isn't just data—it's an emotional experience.

02

Remove friction at every step

Users shouldn't have to leave the app to listen, save, or explore. Keep everything seamless and integrated.

03

Reward engagement, not just identification

Give users reasons to stay by showing them value beyond the initial song lookup—recommendations, playlists, and personalized content.

08 — IMPACT

What we achieved

Our redesign transforms Shazam from a forgettable utility into an engaging music discovery platform. By addressing the "identify and bounce" problem, we created a foundation for long-term user retention and deeper engagement.

"This redesign doesn't just solve a UX problem—it fundamentally changes how users think about Shazam."
